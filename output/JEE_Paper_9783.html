Question 1   Correct Answer: $3.0\text{ km/h}$
Derivation:
In the reference frame moving with the stream (river frame): 1. The raft is stationary. 2. The motorboat moves away from the raft at a relative speed $v_{br}$ for a duration $\tau$. 3. Upon turning back, the motorboat approaches the raft with the same relative speed $v_{br}$. 4. Thus, the return time relative to the raft is also $\tau$, making the total time of travel $T = 2\tau = 2.0\text{ h}$. 5. In the ground frame, the raft is carried by the stream over distance $l = 6.0\text{ km}$ in time $T$. 6. Therefore, stream velocity $v_r = \frac{l}{T} = \frac{6.0\text{ km}}{2.0\text{ h}} = 3.0\text{ km/h}$.
Question 2   Correct Answer: A
Derivation:
During the upward motion, the equation of motion is $m v \frac{dv}{dh} = -(mg + kv^2)$. Separating variables gives $\frac{m v \, dv}{mg + kv^2} = -dh$. Integrating from $v = v_0$ at $h = 0$ to $v = 0$ at $h = h_{\text{max}}$: $\int_{v_0}^{0} \frac{m v \, dv}{mg + kv^2} = -h_{\text{max}}$. Using substitution $u = mg + kv^2$, we get $\frac{m}{2k} \ln\left(\frac{mg}{mg + kv_0^2}\right) = -h_{\text{max}}$, which simplifies to $h_{\text{max}} = \frac{m}{2k} \ln\left(1 + \frac{kv_0^2}{mg}\right)$.
Question 3   Correct Answer: A
Derivation:
Given $x = a \sin(\omega t)$ and $y = a(1 - \cos(\omega t))$. Differentiating with respect to time $t$, we obtain the velocity components: $v_x = \frac{dx}{dt} = a \omega \cos(\omega t)$ and $v_y = \frac{dy}{dt} = a \omega \sin(\omega t)$. The magnitude of the velocity vector is $v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = a \omega \sqrt{\cos^2(\omega t) + \sin^2(\omega t)} = a \omega$. Since the speed $v$ is constant in time, the total distance traversed during time $\tau$ is given by $S = \int_0^\tau v \, dt = a \omega \tau$.

Derivation:

Derivation:
Differentiating the position coordinates with respect to time gives the velocity components: $v_x = \frac{dx}{dt} = a$ $v_y = \frac{dy}{dt} = a(1 - 2\alpha t)$ So, $\vec{v} = a\hat{i} + a(1 - 2\alpha t)\hat{j}$. 2. The cosine of the angle $\theta$ between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{w}$ is: $\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w}}{|\vec{v}||\vec{w}|} = \frac{-2a^2\alpha(1 - 2\alpha t)}{\sqrt{a^2 + a^2(1 - 2\alpha t)^2} \cdot 2a\alpha} = \frac{-(1 - 2\alpha t)}{\sqrt{1 + (1 - 2\alpha t)^2}}$ 4. Setting $\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$, we get $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$: $\frac{-(1 - 2\alpha t)}{\sqrt{1 + (1 - 2\alpha t)^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ Squaring both sides: $2(1 - 2\alpha t)^2 = 1 + (1 - 2\alpha t)^2 \implies (1 - 2\alpha t)^2 = 1$ Solving gives $1 - 2\alpha t = -1 \implies 2\alpha t = 2 \implies t_0 = \frac{1}{\alpha}$ (for $t_0 > 0$).
Question 6   Correct Answer: $R = \frac{a}{2\alpha} [1 + (1 - 2\alpha x/a)^2]^{3/2}$
Derivation:
Express $y$ in terms of $x$: Since $t = x/a$, we have $y = x\left(1 - \frac{\alpha x}{a}\right) = x - \frac{\alpha x^2}{a}$. 2. Substituting the derivatives: $R = \frac{\left[1 + \left(1 - \frac{2\alpha x}{a}\right)^2\right]^{3/2}}{\frac{2\alpha}{a}} = \frac{a}{2\alpha} \left[1 + \left(1 - \frac{2\alpha x}{a}\right)^2\right]^{3/2}$.
Question 7   Correct Answer: $v = \sqrt{gL(1 - \eta^2)}$
Derivation:
Let the table surface be the reference level for potential energy ($U = 0$). 2. Initially, the hanging part of the chain has length $\eta L$ and mass $\eta m$. Its center of mass is located at a depth of $\frac{\eta L}{2}$ below the table. 3. The initial potential energy is $U_i = - (\eta m) g \left(\frac{\eta L}{2}\right) = -\frac{1}{2} \eta^2 m g L$. 4. Since the chain starts from rest, the initial kinetic energy $K_i = 0$. 5. At the moment the chain completely leaves the table, its total length $L$ hangs vertically with its center of mass at a depth of $\frac{L}{2}$ below the table surface. 6. The final potential energy is $U_f = - m g \left(\frac{L}{2}\right) = -\frac{1}{2} m g L$. 7. The final kinetic energy is $K_f = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$. 8. Applying conservation of mechanical energy ($K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f$): $$0 - \frac{1}{2}\eta^2 m g L = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 - \frac{1}{2} m g L$$ $$\frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} m g L (1 - \eta^2) \implies v = \sqrt{gL(1 - \eta^2)}$$

Total time of motion $T = t_1 + 2t_2 = \frac{S}{2} \left( \frac{1}{v_0} + \frac{2}{v_1 + v_2} \right) = \frac{S (v_1 + v_2 + 2v_0)}{2 v_0 (v_1 + v_2)}$. 4. Average velocity $\langle v \rangle = \frac{S}{T} = \frac{2 v_0 (v_1 + v_2)}{v_1 + v_2 + 2v_0}$. 5. Substituting $v_0 = 3.0\text{ m/s}$, $v_1 = 2.0\text{ m/s}$, $v_2 = 8.0\text{ m/s}$: $\langle v \rangle = \frac{2 \times 3.0 \times (2.0 + 8.0)}{2.0 + 8.0 + 2 \times 3.0} = \frac{60}{16} = 3.75\text{ m/s}$.

Derivation:
From $x = a t$, we have $t = \frac{x}{a}$. Substituting $t$ into $y = a t (1 - \alpha t)$, we get $y = x \left(1 - \frac{\alpha x}{a}\right)$. The magnitude of velocity is $v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = a\sqrt{1 + \left(1 - \frac{2\alpha x}{a}\right)^2}$.
